How to create a 3D website: tools, costs and mistakes to avoid

A 3D website can be built yourself, with tools or with an agency. Here is how to choose, what to prepare and the SEO mistakes to avoid.

Does your site need 3D?

A 3D website shows an object, a scene or a place in three dimensions right inside the page: visitors rotate it, zoom in, sometimes configure it. It is useful when seeing the object from every angle helps people decide: a made-to-measure product, a place to visit, a technical part, a set of plans.

If your business can be understood with a few good photos, a fast, well-written classic website will often do better. 3D is a means, not a goal. Before choosing a tool, ask yourself one question: what should the visitor understand or decide thanks to 3D? If you cannot answer in one sentence, 3D is probably not the priority.

Three quick questions to decide: does your product or venue gain from being seen in volume? Do your customers hesitate because they cannot picture it? Do you have, or can you obtain, quality 3D models? If you answer yes to at least two of them, 3D deserves a test; otherwise, focus first on clear content and good photos, which you can complete later.

Four ways to create a 3D website

There is no single ideal method. Each has strengths and limits.

  • A no-code 3D design tool. 3D design apps let you compose a scene and export it to a web page. Strengths: quick start, a visual result without programming. Limits: little control over the weight of the scene, its loading and SEO; the scene often stays an isolated block within the page.
  • A 3D code library. JavaScript libraries such as Three.js display 3D in the browser using a technology called WebGL. Strengths: full control, custom scenes, advanced interactions. Limits: you need to know how to code, and mobile performance demands rigour.
  • A website builder or plugin. Some builders and plugins (WordPress, for example) include a 3D viewer. Strengths: easy to install. Limits: limited models and options, heavy pages, dependence on the plugin and its updates.
  • An agency. It chooses the lightest technology for your need, designs the page around the 3D (text, journey, mobile) and handles launch and follow-up. Strengths: a result consistent with your business and your Google visibility. Limits: a budget to plan.

What each method asks of you

The real cost of a 3D website is not only a price: it is also time and skills.

  • No-code tool: learning time, then maintaining the scene and the page yourself;
  • Code library: web development skills, or a freelance developer to manage;
  • Builder or plugin: handling updates, security and compatibility;
  • Agency: your goals, your content and your approvals; the rest is handled, with a dedicated manager.

If your site must generate enquiries or sales, someone should be responsible for its speed, its SEO and its follow-up over time.

Cases where 3D makes sense

3D adds value when it replaces an explanation or a visit:

  • Made-to-measure or technical products: showing dimensions, materials or options;
  • Real estate and venues: letting people explore a space before visiting;
  • Portfolios: presenting design work in volume, such as an object or packaging;
  • Events: telling the story of a venue or programme in a memorable way;
  • Industry: explaining a machine or an assembly without a technical drawing.

In all these cases, 3D complements the text, it does not replace it. To go further on one of them, read our article on the 3D product configurator.

What to prepare before you start

  1. A precise goal: request a quote, book a visit, buy, understand a product;
  2. Your 3D models: do you already have files (product, plans, mock-ups)? If not, creating them must be planned and priced;
  3. Real written content: titles, descriptions, frequently asked questions. Google reads the text, not the scene;
  4. The journey after the 3D: what does the visitor do next (contact, quote, basket)?
  5. A realistic budget and timeline: the complexity of the scene changes both. For benchmarks, see the cost of a 3D website in Switzerland.

The most common SEO mistakes

  • Text drawn inside the scene: Google cannot read it. Text must stay HTML;
  • 3D that blocks the display: the page stays empty while the scene loads;
  • Models that are too heavy: the page becomes slow, especially on phones;
  • No fallback image: nothing shows if the 3D fails to load;
  • No dedicated page for the offer: a nice scene without a page answering “3D website design” will not rank;
  • Mobile forgotten: an experience designed for large screens that collapses on a phone.

We detail these points in our article 3D websites and SEO: risks and best practices.

Performance checklist before launch

  • The text is readable before the 3D appears;
  • The scene loads after the first display of the page;
  • Models are compressed and the weight of the initial scene is kept under control;
  • A fallback image with fixed dimensions avoids layout shifts;
  • The page is tested on mobile with PageSpeed Insights;
  • The visitor’s “reduce motion” setting is respected.

For reference, Google considers a page “good” when the largest element appears in 2.5 seconds or less (LCP), the page responds in 200 milliseconds or less (INP) and the layout stays stable (CLS of 0.1 or less).

Questions to ask before signing

Whether you choose a freelancer or an agency, these questions let you compare two proposals on solid ground:

  1. Where is the text? It must stay text in the page, not inside the scene;
  2. What does the visitor see while the 3D loads, and if it fails to load?
  3. What weight is targeted for the initial scene and for the full page?
  4. How is the mobile version handled? Lightweight 3D, a fallback image?
  5. Who owns the 3D models and the source files?
  6. Who handles hosting, maintenance and updates after launch?
  7. How is SEO taken into account from the design stage?

A clear proposal answers these points in writing, in the quote. If an answer stays vague, ask for details before committing.

DIY or agency?

Doing it yourself is a good choice for a demo, a personal project or a test. An agency is safer when the website is a tool of your business: it must then be fast, well referenced, maintained and consistent with the rest of your communication.

Key points
  • 3D is a means, not a goal: it should help the visitor understand or decide.
  • Four methods exist: no-code tool, code library, plugin or builder, agency.
  • Text, speed and mobile decide your SEO, not the 3D scene itself.

FAQ

Can you create a 3D website for free?

Some tools offer a free version to test a scene. For a professional website (domain, hosting, SEO, maintenance), you need to plan a budget. See our article on the cost of a 3D website.

Do you need to code to create a 3D website?

Not with a no-code tool, but control over performance and SEO is then limited. With a 3D code library, yes.

Is a 3D website bad for SEO?

No, provided the text stays in the page, the 3D loads after the first display and the models are lightweight.

How long does it take to create a 3D website?

It depends on the scene, the available models and the number of pages. At Websituation, the schedule is calculated at the start of the project.

Does a 3D website work on a phone?

Yes, provided it is designed mobile-first, with lightweight models and a fallback image.
